The AH-64 Apache is one of the most advanced attack helicopters in the world, and Australia has acquired 29 of these lethal aircraft under the AIR 9000 program.

Built by Boeing, the AH-64E Apache Guardian features a powerful 30mm chain gun, Hellfire anti-tank missiles, and 70mm Hydra rockets. Its Longbow radar can track over 128 targets simultaneously from up to 8 kilometers away.

Australia's Apaches are operated by the 1st Aviation Regiment based at Robertson Barracks in Darwin. Their primary role is close air support for ground troops — a capability that proved essential in Afghanistan and Iraq.

The helicopter's defensive systems include radar warning receivers, missile approach warners, and infrared countermeasures. These systems give the Apache a survival advantage in contested airspace.

With a cruise speed of 150 knots and a range of 260 nautical miles, the Apache can operate across the vast distances of northern Australia. Its ability to operate from forward operating bases makes it ideal for Australia's defense strategy in the Indo-Pacific region.

The Apache is a true force multiplier. Its combination of firepower, survivability, and mobility makes it one of the most feared attack helicopters on any battlefield.
