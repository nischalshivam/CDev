The national anthem that night lasted longer than the fight.

Ninety-one seconds.

That was all Mike Tyson needed to answer a question the boxing world had been arguing about for years.

Who was the real heavyweight champion?

Because standing across from Tyson wasn't some washed-up contender looking for a payday.

It was Michael Spinks.

Undefeated.

Thirty-one fights. Thirty-one wins.

A former light-heavyweight champion who had moved up in weight and beaten the great Larry Holmes not once, but twice.

Spinks had never lost a professional fight.

He had never even been knocked down.

And many people still considered him the legitimate heavyweight champion of the world.

But on June 27th, 1988, Michael Spinks was about to discover that fighting Mike Tyson was different from fighting anyone else.

Because by this point, Tyson wasn't simply beating heavyweights.

He was frightening them.

At just 21 years old, Tyson was already the undisputed heavyweight champion.

Thirty-four fights.

Thirty-four victories.

Thirty knockouts.

Trevor Berbick had fallen.

Larry Holmes had fallen.

Tony Tubbs had fallen.

And almost every fighter who entered the ring with Tyson seemed to face the same problem.

Before they could figure out how to beat him...

they first had to convince themselves they weren't afraid of him.

And Tyson knew it.

Years later, describing Spinks that night, Tyson said:

"When I came into the ring, I could see the fear."

But Michael Spinks wasn't supposed to be another terrified opponent.

He was experienced.

He was intelligent.

He was awkward.

And most importantly, he knew how to win.

There was a reason this fight had become one of the biggest events boxing had ever seen.

Two undefeated champions.

Two legitimate claims to the heavyweight throne.

One fight that was supposed to settle everything.

For weeks, people debated what would happen if Spinks could survive Tyson's early attack.

Maybe Tyson would get frustrated.

Maybe Spinks' movement would confuse him.

Maybe the longer the fight went, the better Spinks' chances became.

There was just one problem with that plan.

Michael Spinks had to survive the beginning.

The bell rang.

And Tyson didn't come out looking for information.

He came out looking for Spinks.

Immediately, Tyson moved forward.

No feeling-out process.

No cautious opening round.

Spinks tried to create space, but Tyson kept closing it.

This was what made prime Mike Tyson so terrifying.

He wasn't just powerful.

He was fast enough to deliver that power before opponents had time to react.

Tyson slipped inside.

Spinks tried to hold him.

The referee separated them.

And then Tyson went back to work.

A punch snapped Spinks' head back.

Then Tyson drove a brutal right hand into the body.

Spinks dropped to one knee.

For the first time in his professional career...

Michael Spinks had been knocked down.

And barely a minute had passed.

Think about that.

This was an undefeated champion who had gone twenty rounds with Larry Holmes across two fights.

Tyson needed roughly sixty seconds to put him on the floor.

Spinks got back up.

The referee looked into his eyes.

The fight continued.

And for a few seconds, Michael Spinks had one job.

Survive.

Move.

Hold.

Do anything necessary to slow Tyson down.

Instead, Tyson came straight back at him.

Spinks threw a desperate right hand.

Tyson ducked underneath it.

And suddenly Spinks was completely exposed.

Tyson fired back.

Left.

Then right.

The final right hand landed flush.

Spinks' head snapped back.

His legs disappeared underneath him.

And he crashed onto the canvas.

This time there was no recovery.

Spinks tried to rise.

His body wouldn't cooperate.

The referee counted.

Seven.

Eight.

Nine.

Ten.

It was over.

One minute and thirty-one seconds.

Ninety-one seconds.

Michael Spinks had entered the ring undefeated.

He left with the only loss of his professional career.

He would never fight again.

And Mike Tyson?

He simply stood in the center of the ring with his arms spread as the arena exploded around him.

There was almost no celebration on his face.

It looked less like Tyson had accomplished something impossible...

and more like something had happened exactly the way he expected it to.

That may be the most frightening part of the entire fight.

Everyone else had spent months wondering whether Michael Spinks could beat Mike Tyson.

Tyson seemed to spend ninety-one seconds showing them why they had asked the wrong question.

Because this wasn't the night Mike Tyson became heavyweight champion.

He had already done that.

It wasn't even the night Tyson became undisputed champion.

He had already done that too.

This was something different.

This was the night the mythology finally matched the man.

For years, people had watched Tyson destroy fighters and wondered what would happen when he finally faced someone truly elite.

Someone undefeated.

Someone intelligent.

Someone who knew what it meant to be champion.

Michael Spinks was supposed to provide the answer.

And he did.

Just not the answer anyone expected.

Tyson was three days away from his 22nd birthday.

He had now beaten every major claimant to the heavyweight throne.

And for one brief moment in boxing history, there was almost nothing left to debate.

Mike Tyson wasn't just the champion.

He looked inevitable.

Of course, we know now that Tyson wasn't invincible.

Less than two years later, Buster Douglas would prove that in one of boxing's greatest upsets.

But on this night...

in Atlantic City...

none of that had happened yet.

There was only a 21-year-old heavyweight standing in the center of the ring...

an undefeated champion lying against the ropes...

and an entire sport trying to understand what it had just witnessed.

Months of promotion.

Years of anticipation.

Two undefeated champions.

Ninety-one seconds.

That was all it took.

And for perhaps the most terrifying minute and a half boxing had ever seen...

Mike Tyson looked exactly like the monster everyone believed he was.
